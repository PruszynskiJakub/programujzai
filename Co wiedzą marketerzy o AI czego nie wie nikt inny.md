This video features Rory Sutherland, Vice Chairman of Ogilvy UK, sharing his predictions for 2026. He focuses on how AI and automation are being integrated into the business world, specifically highlighting the risks of focusing on cost reduction at the expense of human value and user experience.

**The "Doorman Fallacy" and AI**

A central theme is the **"Doorman Fallacy"** [[01:07](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=67)]. Sutherland explains that businesses often replace a human role (like a doorman) with a mechanical one (an automatic door) to save costs. While the "cost-saving" is easy to measure on a spreadsheet, the "value destroyed" is often invisible:

- **Intangible Value:** A doorman provides security, recognizes regulars, and hails taxis—functions a machine cannot replicate.
- **Asymmetric Accountability:** Consultants and finance departments often take credit for the savings but are rarely held accountable for the long-term destruction of brand value or customer trust [[02:51](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=171)].

**The Three Phases of AI Evolution**

Sutherland predicts that AI's influence on business and UX will move through three distinct stages:

1. **The "Same, but Cheaper" Phase:** The current trend where AI is used primarily to reduce headcount and drive users toward low-cost, automated channels [[12:49](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=769)].
2. **The "Same, but Better" Phase:** A shift toward using AI to actually enhance the customer experience rather than just cutting costs [[13:03](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=783)].
3. **The Reinvention Phase:** A complete rethinking of business processes. He uses the analogy of the electric motor: initial gains were small until factories were entirely redesigned around the new technology [[13:55](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=835)].

**Key Insights for UX and Marketing**

- **Quantification Bias:** Businesses focus on what is easy to measure (speed, cost) and ignore what is hard to measure (trust, human perception) [[09:16](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=556)]. This often leads to "efficient" systems that are frustrating for users.
- **Human Element as a Premium:** Sutherland argues that as automation becomes ubiquitous, the human element of an exchange will become a disproportionate driver of customer satisfaction [[08:02](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=482)].
- **Efficiency vs. Discovery:** Most businesses view themselves as "efficiency mechanisms." Sutherland argues they should instead be "discovery mechanisms," using AI to explore new ways to create subjective value for users [[06:34](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=394)].
- **Proactive UX/Marketing:** In the future, the lower cost of AI-driven production might allow agencies to create content proactively and find buyers later, rather than waiting for a client brief [[16:31](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=991)].

**Final Takeaway for Designers**

The real value of UX and marketing professionals is not just "what they do" but "how they think" [[18:05](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=1085)]. Sutherland emphasizes that without a "marketer in the room" to advocate for the human perspective, rational engineering decisions can lead to "seriously dumb" outcomes—optimizing for metrics that don't actually matter to people [[20:25](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6SXCJhqXubU&t=1225)].
